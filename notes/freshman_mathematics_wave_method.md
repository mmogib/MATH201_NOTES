# How to Study Freshman Mathematics: The Wave Method

You may study Freshman Mathematics in **waves**, not all at once.

## Wave One: Immediately After Each Class

After the lecture, you need to:

- Solve **all examples** from the section discussed in that lecture.
  - Make sure to solve them **without reading the solution first**.
- Solve some exercises at the end of the section **from the suggested problems and more**.
- Do this **for every section**, consistently.

This wave builds **first understanding and familiarity**.

## Wave Two: At the End of the Week

At the end of the week, you need to:

- Solve **more exercises** at the end of each section discussed that week.
- Collaborate with a classmate to test you with exercises from these sections **without telling you where they come from**.

This wave builds **retention and flexibility**.

## Wave Three: After a Chapter or Before a Quiz

After completing a chapter, or before a quiz, you need to:

- Solve **more exercises** at the end of each section from the chapter or the material included in the quiz.
- Collaborate with a classmate to test you with mixed exercises from these sections **without telling you where they come from**.
- If you have time, solve some old exams.

This wave builds **integration and readiness**.

## Wave Four: Before a Major Exam

Before a major exam, you need to:

- Work under **exam-like conditions**:
  - Use a timer.
  - Do not use notes.
  - Avoid interruptions.
- Solve:
  - Selected **old exams**.
  - Mixed problems covering **all relevant topics**.
- After each practice session:
  - Identify your **patterns of mistakes**.
  - Write a short list of:
    - Things you still confuse.
    - Formulas or ideas you misuse.
- Redo the problems you got wrong **one or two days later**, from scratch.

This wave builds **accuracy, speed, and confidence**.

## Very Important Note

**ALWAYS ASK QUESTIONS.**

If something is unclear at any wave, do not wait for the next one.
